What are Binary Numbers?

1. The Base-2 System

Humans count in Base-10 (Decimal), likely because we have ten fingers. We use digits 0-9. Computers, however, use Base-2 (Binary). They only have two states: 0 (OFF) and 1 (ON). This is because computer processors are made of billions of tiny transistors that act as simple light switches.

2. How the Math Works

In Decimal, each place value is a power of 10 (1, 10, 100). In Binary, each place value is a power of 2. To find the value of a binary number, you simply add up the values of the "ON" bits.

Example: The binary number 00001010 is 8 + 2, which equals 10 in decimal.

3. Bits and Bytes

A single 0 or 1 is called a Bit. A group of 8 bits (like the odometer above) is called a Byte. One byte can represent any number from 0 up to 255. When the odometer hits 255 and you add 1, it "overflows" and rolls back to 0!

4. Why This Matters

Everything you see on a computer—photos, videos, and games—is actually just a massive sequence of these 1s and 0s being processed millions of times per second.
